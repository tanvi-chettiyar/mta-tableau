Headline
Penn Station handles ~2.9M paid entries a month. When a Signal failure hits — and one does in 11 of 12 months — the cost is real: ~3,400 trains delayed across 2024, a median of 19 trains per incident, and a single worst hour (Tuesday 8 AM) that costs riders 3.2× more than an average weekday. This dashboard quantifies that cost.

So what: the 1/2/3 complex absorbs ~35% more delay than the A/C/E complex (1,950 vs 1,440 trains). The cost concentrates on one platform — the same one where Wait Assessment is lowest.
The bottom line
The cost of a Signal failure at Penn isn't an abstraction — it's 19 trains delayed in the median case, 52 minutes in the worst, and 3.2× more if it happens at 8 AM Tuesday. Multiply that by 11 months of failures and ~2.9M monthly entries, and "reliability" stops being a metric and becomes a rider's everyday calculation. The data tells riders when to brace and operators where to invest.
Caveats: 2024 weekday data only. "Trains delayed" measures incidents that caused at least one train delay; it doesn't capture cancelled trains or post-incident schedule recovery. Severity proxy (delays/incident) treats all delayed trains as equivalent.
